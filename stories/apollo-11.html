THE MISSION RECORD

Apollo 11 Descent Stage

Dates
Landed 20 Jul 1969. Left 21 Jul 1969.
Location
Tranquility Base, Sea of Tranquility · 0.67416° N, 23.47314° E.
What happened
Eagle’s lower half stayed behind when the ascent stage lifted the crew into lunar orbit.
Fun fact
It still carries the plaque commemorating the first crewed Moon landing.

Why it stayed

The lower stage carried the landing gear and descent engine. The upper stage had its own engine for leaving the surface, so the crew could depart without lifting the landing hardware.

What the evidence shows

Look for the legged shape in the orbital record. That shape marks the landing stage.

Time on the surface

Armstrong and Aldrin spent 21 hours and 36 minutes on the lunar surface. The crew splashed down on Earth on 24 July 1969.

Read the transcript
  1. Eagle lands

    On 20 July 1969, Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed Eagle at Tranquility Base. The descent engine slowed the lunar module, and the landing gear supported it on the surface.

  2. Two stages, two jobs

    On 21 July, the ascent stage lifted the astronauts off the Moon. The descent stage served as its launch platform and stayed at Tranquility Base.

  3. The view from orbit

    In 2009, NASA’s Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter photographed the landing site. The descent stage can be identified by its position and shadow.
