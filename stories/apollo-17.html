  1. A vehicle for Taurus–Littrow

    Apollo 17 landed in Taurus–Littrow on 11 December 1972. The electric rover carried the astronauts, tools and samples across the valley.

  2. A final parking spot

    After the last drive, the crew parked the rover away from Challenger so its camera had a clear view of the lunar module.

  3. The camera keeps rolling

    On 14 December, the rover’s television camera recorded the ascent stage lifting off. The rover stayed where the crew parked it.

THE MISSION RECORD

Apollo 17 Lunar Rover

Dates
Landed 11 Dec 1972. Left 14 Dec 1972.
Location
Taurus–Littrow Valley · 20.1896° N, 30.7769° E (final parking spot).
What happened
The crew parked the rover and left it on the Moon when they departed.
Fun fact
Its TV camera recorded the lunar module lifting off.

Why the rover stayed parked

The rover carried people, equipment and a television camera across the valley. At the end, it gave the camera a viewpoint away from the lunar module.

What the evidence shows

The listed coordinates identify the rover’s final parking spot. The interactive trail is an illustration of the journey.
