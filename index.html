Sea of Tranquility · 1969

A mirror on the Moon

Apollo 11 left a panel of 100 small reflectors on the lunar surface. Decades later, scientists can still use it to measure the Moon’s distance.

Why leave it there?

Armstrong and Aldrin set the reflector on the Moon so observatories on Earth could send light to a known point on the lunar surface.

How does the light come back?

Each reflector is shaped like the inside corner of a cube. The shape sends some of the incoming light back toward the observatory that fired it. The panel needs no electricity.

What did it help scientists learn?

Repeated measurements help scientists track changes in the Moon’s orbit and rotation.

Meridiani Planum · 2004–2018

Opportunity’s last signal

A solar-powered rover planned for 90 Martian days kept exploring for nearly 15 Earth years. Its final transmission came as a dust storm darkened the sky.

What was it looking for?

Opportunity studied rocks and soil to learn whether Mars once had liquid water. Its observations at Eagle Crater helped show that water once affected the rocks there.

Why did the signal stop?

The 2018 dust storm blocked sunlight from reaching its solar panels. The rover last contacted its team on June 10, 2018. NASA ended recovery attempts in February 2019.

What remains?

The rover rests in Perseverance Valley. Its images and measurements still give researchers a record of the terrain it crossed and the rocks it studied.
